Chapter 1: Foundations of Spanish Grammar & Regular Present Tense
Spanish is a Romance language characterized by grammatical gender (masculine and feminine), inflected verb
conjugations according to person and number, and null-subject flexibility (pro-drop). In the present indicative tense,
verbs belong to one of three inflectional classes ending in -ar, -er, or -ir.

1.1 Regular Conjugation Patterns
For regular verbs, remove the infinitive suffix and append person-number morphemes:
- Hablar (-ar): yo hablo, tu hablas, el/ella/usted habla, nosotros hablamos, vosotros hablais, ellos/ustedes hablan.
- Comer (-er): yo como, tu comes, el/ella/usted come, nosotros comemos, vosotros comeis, ellos/ustedes comen.
- Vivir (-ir): yo vivo, tu vives, el/ella/usted vive, nosotros vivimos, vosotros vivis, ellos/ustedes viven.

1.2 Epistemic Distinction: Grammatical Rule vs Pragmatic Usage
Subject pronouns (yo, tu, etc.) are frequently omitted in standard Spanish because verb inflections encode grammatical
person unambiguously. Pronouns are explicitly supplied for emphasis, contrast, or disambiguation.

Chapter 2: The Existential & State Distinction: Ser vs. Estar
Unlike English which employs the single copular verb 'to be', Spanish maintains an essential epistemic and ontological
dichotomy between Ser (inherent nature, identity, classification) and Estar (state, condition, spatial location).

2.1 Core Domain of Ser
Ser (conjugation: soy, eres, es, somos, sois, son) is used for:
1. Identity, essence, and nationality (Soy arquitecto; Carlos es espanol).
2. Origin and material composition (La mesa es de madera).
3. Time, date, and temporal measurements (Son las tres de la tarde; Hoy es lunes).
4. Location of events (El concierto es en el estadio).

2.2 Core Domain of Estar
Estar (conjugation: estoy, estas, esta, estamos, estais, estan) is used for:
1. Spatial location of physical entities (El libro esta sobre la mesa; Madrid esta en Espana).
2. Temporary physical or emotional states (Estoy cansado; Maria esta contenta).
3. Ongoing actions in continuous aspect (Estamos estudiando gramatica espanola).
4. Resulting states produced by an action (La puerta esta cerrada).

2.3 Meaning Shifts Based on Copular Selection
The selection of Ser vs Estar can alter the meaning of qualifying adjectives:
- Ser listo = to be smart / clever; Estar listo = to be ready / prepared.
- Ser verde = to be green in color; Estar verde = to be unripe / inexperienced.
- Ser aburrido = to be boring (intrinsic trait); Estar aburrido = to be bored (transient state).

Stem-changing verbs undergo vocalic alternation in the root syllable when that syllable receives grammatical stress (in
yo, tu, el/ella, and ellos forms). The nosotros and vosotros forms remain unshifted.

3.1 Three Primary Alternation Classes
1. e -> ie alternation: Pensar (pienso, piensas, piensa, pensamos, pensais, piensan); Entender, Cerrar, Querer.
2. o -> ue alternation: Dormir (duermo, duermes, duerme, dormimos, dormis, duermen); Poder, Volver, Encontrar.
3. e -> i alternation (-ir verbs only): Pedir (pido, pides, pide, pedimos, pedis, piden); Servir, Repetir.

Chapter 4: The Past Tense Frontier: Preterite vs. Imperfect Aspect
In the indicative past, Spanish distinguishes not between temporal distance, but between grammatical aspect: the
Preterite (perfective aspect: completed event with definite bounds) vs. Imperfect (imperfective aspect: ongoing, habitual,
background, or unbounded).

4.1 The Preterite (Preterito Indefinido)
Denotes actions viewed as single completed wholes in past time:
- Regular -ar: hable, hablaste, hablo, hablamos, hablasteis, hablaron.
- Regular -er/-ir: comi, comiste, comio, comimos, comisteis, comieron.
- Common irregular stems: tener -> tuve, estar -> estuve, hacer -> hice/hizo, poner -> puse, poder -> pude, saber ->
supe.

4.2 The Imperfect (Preterito Imperfecto)
Denotes continuous past states, background descriptions, habitual actions ('used to'), and age/time in the past:
- Regular -ar: hablaba, hablabas, hablaba, hablabamos, hablabais, hablaban.
- Regular -er/-ir: comia, comias, comia, comiamos, comiais, comian.
- Irregular verbs in Imperfect: Only three exist: ser (era), ir (iba), and ver (veia).

4.3 Critical Semantic Aspect Shifts in Stative Verbs
Certain stative verbs take on distinct aspectual interpretations in the preterite vs imperfect:
- Conocer: Imperfect 'conocía' = I knew / was acquainted with; Preterite 'conocí' = I met (inception of knowing).
- Saber: Imperfect 'sabía' = I knew (ongoing state); Preterite 'supe' = I found out / discovered (inceptive realization).
- Querer: Imperfect 'quería' = I wanted (mental state); Preterite 'quise' = I attempted/tried; 'no quise' = I refused.
- Poder: Imperfect 'podía' = I was able / had the capability; Preterite 'pude' = I managed to / succeeded in doing.

Chapter 5: The Subjunctive Mood: Epistemic Unreality & Volition
The Subjunctive is a grammatical mood expressing unreality, subjectivity, volition, doubt, and non-assertion. It requires
both an activating matrix clause trigger (WEIRDO: Wishes, Emotions, Impersonal observations, Recommendations,
Doubt, Ojalá) and a change of grammatical subject between matrix and embedded clauses.

5.1 Present Subjunctive Morphological Rule
Morphological algorithm: Take the first-person singular indicative (yo) form in present tense, drop the terminal -o, and
apply opposite vowel endings:
- -ar verbs adopt -e endings: hable, hables, hable, hablemos, hableis, hablen.
- -er/-ir verbs adopt -a endings: coma, comas, coma, comamos, comais, coman; viva, vivas, viva, vivamos, vivan.
- Irregular yo-roots are preserved: tener -> teng- -> tenga; hacer -> hag- -> haga; decir -> dig- -> diga.
- Irregulars with no -o root: sea (ser), este (estar), vaya (ir), sepa (saber), haya (haber), de (dar).
